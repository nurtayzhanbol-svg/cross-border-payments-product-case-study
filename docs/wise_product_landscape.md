## Pricing changes and stated priorities

- **Fee reviews.** Feb 2025: average fee fell from 0.67% to 0.59%; "smaller transfers may now be more expensive" on some routes because the variable fee fell while the fixed fee rose. May 2024: some routes "got cheaper for larger transfers and more expensive for smaller transfers". Sources: https://wise.com/gb/blog/feb-25-fee-review-summary ; https://wise.com/gb/blog/were-updating-our-fees-may-2024 ; https://wise.com/gb/blog/a-summary-of-fees-june-24
- **FY2026 results (25 June 2026).** 18.9m active customers (+21%), cross-border volume $243.5bn (+31%), average take rate 52 bps, 75% of Q4 payments completed in under 20 seconds. New licences (South Africa, UAE, Thailand), direct connections (Brazil, Japan), Wise Platform partners (UniCredit, Raiffeisen, MBSB, Capitec). Source: https://owners.wise.com/news-releases/news-release-details/wise-group-plc-reports-full-year-2026-financial-results

## Implications for this case study

1. Transparency features (mid-market rate, fee breakdown, comparison) **already exist**. They are not proposed as new.
2. Pricing benefits currently reward **large monthly volume** (≥ £20k / $25k). Nothing public rewards **frequent small senders** (e.g. £200 a month), the typical remittance pattern. This gap is the basis of the selected hypothesis.
3. Recurring sends already exist as scheduled transfers, so a recurring *mechanism* is not new; a *price* tied to recurring small sends would be.
4. Cash pickup is the main payout method Wise does not appear to offer. Adding it would be a large operational and compliance change (agent networks).
5. Wise reprices often, so any RPW-based gap must be re-checked against current prices before acting.
